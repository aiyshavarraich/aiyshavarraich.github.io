About

I am a political scientist who studies corruption, clientelism, and the role of religion in politics, with a focus on South Asia. I am a Lecturer at Georgia Southern University, where I teach Global Issues. I received my PhD in Political Science from the University of Gothenburg in 2024. My dissertation, Clients of God, examines how hierarchical religious bonds shape political behaviour.

My earlier work addresses how corruption is defined and measured. With Bo Rothstein, I wrote Making Sense of Corruption (Cambridge University Press, 2017). With Ina Kubbe, I co-edited Corruption and Informal Practices in the Middle East and North Africa (Routledge, 2020). I served as guest editor of the European Journal of Social Psychology special issue on the social psychology of corruption.

I have conducted fieldwork in Pakistan (2011, 2015, 2019) and Sweden (2010). From 2020 to 2023, I was a Research Associate in the Anti-Corruption Evidence programme at the University of Nottingham and Global Integrity. I was lead organiser of the Interdisciplinary Corruption Research Network Forums in Gothenburg (2018), online (2021), and Barcelona (2023).

Research interests: corruption, political behaviour, religion and politics, gender, South Asia.
